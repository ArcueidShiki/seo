How to calculate position size (formula)

Risk-based position sizing fixes the dollar loss you accept before you enter a trade, then works backwards to the share count:

dollar risk     = account size × risk %
risk per share  = |entry price − stop-loss price|
position size   = dollar risk ÷ risk per share   (round down)

Worked example

A $10,000 account risking 1% on a $50 stock with a $48 stop
StepCalculationResult
Dollar risk$10,000 × 1%$100
Risk per share$50 − $48$2
Position size$100 ÷ $250 shares
Position value50 × $50$2,500 (25% of account)

If the stop is hit you lose about $100 (plus slippage and fees), no matter how far away the stop is — a wider stop simply means fewer shares. Read the full position sizing rules guide for how to choose the risk percentage and stop distance.

Why size positions by risk instead of by dollar amount?

  • Losses stay comparable. Every losing trade costs roughly the same fraction of your account, so one bad trade cannot wipe out weeks of gains.
  • Volatility is built in. Volatile stocks need wider stops, which automatically reduces the share count.
  • Results become measurable. When each trade risks "1R", you can track performance in R-multiples and compare strategies fairly.

The approach is a standard part of trading risk management; TradingView's education page on position sizing covers the same principle.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate position size?

Multiply your account size by the percentage you are willing to risk, then divide by the distance between your entry price and stop-loss price. The result is the number of shares to buy.

What percentage of my account should I risk per trade?

Many risk-management frameworks cap the loss on a single trade at 0.5%–2% of account equity so that a losing streak does not cause a large drawdown. Choose a figure that fits your strategy's win rate and volatility.

Does the calculator work for short positions?

Yes. The calculator uses the absolute distance between entry and stop, so a stop above the entry (short trade) gives the same share count logic.

Does position size change when I use leverage or margin?

No. Risk-based sizing is set by the dollar amount you can lose at the stop. Leverage only changes how much buying power is needed, which the calculator shows as position value.
