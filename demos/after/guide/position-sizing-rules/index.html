Position Sizing Rules: 1% Risk, Stop Distance & Examples

By the Vexlora team · Updated {{UPDATED_HUMAN}} · 6 min read

TL;DR: Decide the maximum dollar loss first (commonly 0.5–2% of equity), place the stop where the trade idea is proven wrong, then divide the dollar loss by the stop distance to get the share count. Use the Vexlora position size calculator to do the arithmetic.

Rule 1 — Fix the risk percentage before you look at the chart

Choosing the risk first stops you from sizing up on trades you "feel good" about. A fixed fraction such as 1% means ten consecutive losses cost about 9.6% of the account (compounding), which is recoverable.

Risk per tradeEquity left after 10 straight losses
0.5%95.1%
1%90.4%
2%81.7%
5%59.9%

Rule 2 — Put the stop where the setup fails, not where the loss feels small

A stop belongs below the level that invalidates the idea — for a breakout, typically below the breakout day's low. Moving the stop closer just to buy more shares raises the chance of being shaken out by normal noise.

Rule 3 — Let volatility shrink the position

Wide stops on volatile stocks automatically produce smaller positions. Some traders set the stop distance as a multiple of the Average True Range (ATR), for example 1.5 × ATR, so sizing adapts to each stock.

Rule 4 — Cap the position value as well as the risk

Tight stops can produce very large positions. Add a second limit — for example no single position above 25% of the account — so a gap through your stop cannot do outsized damage.

Rule 5 — Account for fees, slippage and gaps

Your real loss can exceed the planned risk when price gaps past the stop. Treat the calculated risk as a minimum and keep a buffer, especially around earnings releases.
